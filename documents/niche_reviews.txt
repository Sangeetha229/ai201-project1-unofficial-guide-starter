Department: Public Health
Date: 2024-12-15
Review: Texas A&M has a genuinely strong pre-med and public health program but the quality of your education depends enormously on which professors you get. The School of Public Health has a handful of truly exceptional professors — Barry, Clendenin, Colwell — and some who are less effective in the classroom. The BIMS program is rigorous and prepares you well for MCAT biology content if you get into Herman's section. SI sessions and the university writing center are underused resources that make a real difference in hard courses.

Department: Public Health
Date: 2024-11-01
Review: SPH at TAMU is a solid program with real research opportunities. The Health Behavior department has the best professors in terms of teaching quality. Adam Barry makes public health genuinely interesting and his courses are the ones I remember most clearly two years later. The Epidemiology department is harder but Clendenin specifically prepares you to read and produce research in a way that helps in med school interviews when they ask about public health experience. Do your research before registration — it matters a lot.

Department: Chemistry
Date: 2025-01-08
Review: Chemistry at TAMU is difficult but survivable if you get the right professor for Organic Chemistry. Goodey for CHEM 227 is the consensus best choice for pre-med students. She is the professor where attending lecture, doing the practice exams, and going to office hours actually translates directly into exam performance. I went from failing the first exam to finishing with a B. The curve at semester end is real and significant. Do not let the early exam scores discourage you if you are in her section.

Source: Niche
Department: BIMS
Date: 2024-10-20
Review: BIMS 301 with Herman is one of the hardest undergraduate courses I took at TAMU and also one of the most valuable. He teaches cell biology and physiology at the depth that medical schools expect, not the surface level you get in some sections. My MCAT practice scores in the biological sciences section went up 8 points after finishing his course. His office hours are the most useful thing I did as a pre-med. Do not take BIMS 301 in the same semester as Orgo.

Department: Public Health
Date: 2025-02-20
Review: The epidemiology sequence at TAMU SPH is genuinely excellent preparation for public health careers and medical school. Clendenin for EPIB 301 is the professor to take — her writing feedback is detailed, her rubrics are clear, and she teaches you to engage with primary research literature not just textbook summaries. The workload is heavy but proportional to what you get out of it. By the end of the semester you can read a CDC epidemiological bulletin and understand what it means.

Department: Public Health
Date: 2024-09-14
Review: Barry for HLTH 320 health behavior theory was the course that convinced me I made the right choice with my public health major. He brings in speakers from Austin and Houston public health departments, assigns real community health reports as reading material, and his exams test whether you can apply theory to actual public health problems. The class is full of pre-med students who often treat it as a GPA boost — it is not an easy A, but if you engage seriously it is incredibly worthwhile.

Source: Niche
Department: Pre-Med
Date: 2025-01-30
Review: Pre-med at TAMU is competitive but the resources are there if you use them. OPSA advisors are helpful and honest about your application profile. The pre-med society runs professor review sessions before registration that are genuinely useful. The key insight after three years: the professor matters more than the reputation of the course. A hard course with a good professor who supports you is better for your application and your MCAT than an easy course with a professor who does not engage with students.
