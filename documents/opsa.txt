Source: TAMU OPSA
Department: Pre-Med
Content: The TAMU Office of Professional School Advising supports students pursuing medicine, dentistry, pharmacy, veterinary medicine, optometry, and other health professional programs. We strongly recommend that students begin formal advising appointments no later than the beginning of their sophomore year. Early advising allows students to build a realistic four-year course plan that completes all prerequisites on schedule while maintaining a competitive GPA. Most competitive medical school applicants have a science GPA of 3.5 or above. Professor selection in prerequisite courses is one of the most consequential decisions a pre-med student makes.

Content: Required prerequisite courses for medical school at TAMU. General Biology with lab: BIOL 111 and BIOL 112. General Chemistry with lab: CHEM 107 and CHEM 117. Organic Chemistry with lab: CHEM 227 and CHEM 237. Biochemistry: BICH 410. Physics with lab: PHYS 201 and PHYS 202. Statistics: STAT 302 or equivalent. English composition: already satisfied by university requirements. Psychology: PSYC 107. Sociology: SOCI 207. Most medical schools also recommend or require one course in public health, ethics, or social determinants of health. HLTH 215 Introduction to Public Health meets this recommendation at TAMU.

Source: TAMU OPSA
Department: Pre-Med
Content: OPSA guidance on Organic Chemistry professor selection. CHEM 227 Organic Chemistry 1 is one of the most grade-sensitive courses in the pre-med pathway. The course is offered in multiple sections with different instructors. We advise students to research all available sections before registering. Student feedback collected through our office consistently identifies significant variation in student outcomes across sections. Students report that access to practice exams, curve policies, and office hours availability vary substantially between instructors. We encourage students to consult with the pre-med society peer mentors and review available student feedback before registering.

Source: TAMU OPSA
Department: BIMS
Content: OPSA guidance on BIMS 301 and 302. The Biomedical Sciences course sequence is among the most important prerequisite coursework for medical school at TAMU. BIMS 301 covers cell biology and genetics at the level of depth expected by medical school curricula. BIMS 302 covers physiology and biochemistry integration. Students who perform well in these courses demonstrate to medical school admissions committees that they are prepared for the academic demands of the first year. We recommend that students take BIMS 301 and CHEM 227 in separate semesters given the intensity of both courses. Discuss course sequencing with your OPSA advisor.

Source: TAMU OPSA
Department: Pre-Med
Content: OPSA guidance on letters of recommendation for medical school. Strong recommendation letters come from professors who know you well as a student, not just professors in whose courses you received high grades. Students who attend office hours regularly, participate in class, and demonstrate intellectual curiosity typically receive the most effective letters. Among frequently requested recommenders from our advisees, faculty in the School of Public Health, the BIMS program, and Chemistry are common sources. Plan your recommendation letter strategy early — strong letters require relationship-building over an entire semester or more.

Source: TAMU OPSA
Department: Pre-Med
Content: OPSA guidance on public health coursework for pre-med students. Medical schools increasingly value applicants who demonstrate understanding of the social determinants of health, health inequities, and population health. TAMU students have access to excellent public health coursework through the School of Public Health. The HLTH minor or a selection of public health electives can strengthen both your application and your clinical reasoning skills. EPIB 301 Introduction to Epidemiology is particularly valued by medical school admissions committees as evidence of quantitative reasoning skills. SPH faculty in the Health Behavior and Epidemiology departments are also known for writing strong recommendation letters for pre-med students who distinguish themselves in their courses.
