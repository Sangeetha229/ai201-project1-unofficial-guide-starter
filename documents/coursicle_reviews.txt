Professor: Adam Barry
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 320
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-11-10
Review: Barry for HLTH 320 is the best professor in SPH. Go to every lecture without exception — his exams draw heavily from in-class discussion and examples he uses that are not in the slides. He posts lecture recordings but they do not capture the full experience. Exams are three in total, all mixture of multiple choice and short answer. He posts a study guide for each exam that closely matches the actual test. Attendance is taken and matters for your participation grade. Easily the most organized course I took at TAMU.

Professor: Adam Barry
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 425
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-10-01
Review: Community Health Assessment with Barry is a project-heavy course. Most of your grade comes from a semester-long group project where you conduct an actual community needs assessment for a real organization in the Bryan-College Station area. The experience looks excellent on med school applications and he writes extremely detailed recommendation letters for students who do well. Choose your group carefully — the project is substantial. His feedback on the written components is detailed and genuinely helpful.

Professor: Joanna Goodey
Department: Chemistry
Course: CHEM 227
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-09-30
Review: Goodey for CHEM 227 — the professor to take for Orgo 1 at TAMU. Her practice exams are essentially the real exam with different numbers on the problems. She provides them two weeks before each test. Office hours before exams are standing room only so go early in the semester to establish a connection. She curves at the end of the semester based on class performance — last year the curve was 9 points. Take the afternoon section, the 8am section moves at a pace that is hard to keep up with before coffee.

Professor: Joanna Goodey
Department: Chemistry
Course: CHEM 237
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2025-01-20
Review: Goodey continues to be excellent in Orgo 2. The spectroscopy unit in the middle of the semester is where most students struggle — go to office hours specifically during weeks 8 through 11. She introduces the material well but spectroscopy problems require a lot of practice that goes beyond just understanding the lecture. Her TAs for 237 are as strong as the ones for 227. Same curve policy as 227. If you finished 227 with her the transition to 237 is smooth.

Professor: Jim Herman
Department: BIMS
Course: BIMS 301
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2025-01-25
Review: Herman for BIMS 301 — what you need to know. His exams are application-heavy. You will not pass by memorizing definitions. You need to understand why biological processes work the way they do and be able to apply that reasoning to novel scenarios. Office hours are small and productive — he limits enrollment so go early in the semester before they fill up. He curved the final exam by 8 points last semester. Records all lectures. Highly valuable for MCAT preparation especially the cell signaling and genetics units.

Professor: Angela Clendenin
Department: Public Health
Course: EPIB 301
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-11-15
Review: Clendenin for EPIB 301 is the most rigorous undergraduate course I took in SPH and probably worth more than any other course for public health careers. She assigns actual epidemiological studies from NEJM and MMWR as required reading — you will learn to critically evaluate research design by the end of the semester. Three exams plus two major papers. She provides incredibly detailed feedback on drafts if you submit early. Her grading standards are high but consistent and fully transparent from week one.

Professor: Brian Colwell
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 310
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-09-18
Review: Colwell for Health Communication is the course I recommend most to pre-med students in the public health minor. Understanding health communication and patient education theory is directly relevant to being a doctor and he connects the coursework to clinical applications throughout. Very accessible professor, responds to emails same day, office hours are never crowded because not enough students take advantage of them. One major research project, two exams, several short papers. Grading is fair and clearly rubric-based.

Professor: Joy DeLeon
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 215
Source: Coursicle
Date: 2024-08-25
Review: DeLeon for HLTH 215 intro to public health. Honest assessment: survivable but not enjoyable. Her slides are extremely dense and she covers them at a pace that makes detailed note-taking difficult. Exam questions sometimes test content she mentioned only in passing. The textbook is more useful than lecture for this course. Form a study group early — that is how most students in her section actually learn the material. She is more available during office hours than her reputation suggests but her explanation style does not always clarify things.
