Professor: Adam Barry
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 320
Source: Uloop
Date: 2024-10-15
Review: Barry is the gold standard for public health professors at TAMU. He connects every lecture to real-world public health applications, brings in practitioners as guest speakers, and makes health behavior theory feel relevant rather than abstract. Exams are challenging but fair — everything is from lecture and he provides study guides. His office hours are consistently the most useful I have attended at TAMU. Sections fill within minutes of registration opening. Set a calendar reminder.

Professor: Adam Barry
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 215
Source: Uloop
Date: 2024-08-22
Review: Barry for intro to public health was my first college course and it set the bar for what a good professor looks like. He explains the why behind every concept, not just the what. He is generous with his time and treats students as intelligent adults capable of engaging with complex material. The course introduced me to epidemiology, health behavior theory, health policy, and environmental health in a way that actually made me want to learn more about each. Excellent foundation course.

Professor: Joy DeLeon
Department: Public Health
Course: HLTH 215
Source: Uloop
Date: 2024-09-01
Review: DeLeon for HLTH 215 is not a professor I would recommend to pre-med students who need to perform well in their prerequisites. Her lecture pace is very fast, her slides are dense with content she does not always explain clearly, and her exams include material covered briefly or not at all in class. She clearly has deep knowledge of the subject but translating that knowledge into effective teaching is a different skill. Expect to spend a lot of time with the textbook to fill gaps from lecture.

Professor: Joanna Goodey
Department: Chemistry
Course: CHEM 227
Source: Uloop
Date: 2024-11-05
Review: Goodey for CHEM 227 is the professor recommendation every returning student gives to every incoming pre-med. Her teaching method works: systematic coverage of mechanisms, practice exams that match the real thing, office hours that are genuinely helpful not performative. She curved the class by 9 points last semester. Her TAs are well-trained and hold regular review sessions. If you are pre-med at TAMU and you do not take Goodey for Orgo 1 you are making your life harder than it needs to be.

Professor: Angela Clendenin
Department: Public Health
Course: EPIB 301
Source: Uloop
Date: 2025-01-12
Review: Clendenin for epidemiology is the most rigorous undergraduate course in SPH and worth every hour of work. She assigns real epidemiological studies as required reading — NEJM, MMWR, CDC reports — and teaches you to critically evaluate research design. Her written feedback on papers is paragraph-level, detailed, and genuinely useful for improving your scientific writing. Her grading rubric is posted week one and does not change. Expect 10 to 12 hours per week. The preparation for public health careers and MCAT research reasoning is excellent.

Professor: Jim Herman
Department: BIMS
Course: BIMS 301
Source: Uloop
Date: 2025-02-08
Review: Herman for BIMS 301 is the pre-med course that separates students who are preparing for medical school from students who are just completing requirements. His exams require application of concepts to novel scenarios — you cannot pass by memorizing definitions. Office hours are small and targeted because he limits enrollment — go from week one. He curved the final exam by 8 points. His cell signaling, genetics, and physiology units directly overlap with MCAT biological sciences content in a way that other sections do not.
