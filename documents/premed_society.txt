Content: The TAMU Pre-Medical Society was founded in 1975 and works closely with the Office of Professional School Advising to support students applying to medical, dental, and other health professional schools. We maintain a professor recommendation database updated each semester by upperclassmen members. Our annual Course Planning Night event brings together juniors and seniors to share professor advice with freshmen and sophomores before registration. Members consistently identify professor selection as one of the most important decisions in the pre-med pathway.

Content: Semester professor recommendations from our Fall 2024 member survey. Chemistry 227 Organic Chemistry 1 — Goodey is the overwhelming consensus choice among our members. She provides practice exams, curves generously, and her teaching style works well for pre-med students who need to move through the material efficiently. BIMS 301 Biomedical Sciences — Herman for students with strong GPAs who prioritize MCAT preparation, Ory for students who need to protect GPA. Both are competent professors but differ significantly in difficulty and depth.

Date: 2025-02-01
Content: Advice from our pre-med mentor program on course sequencing. Do not take Organic Chemistry 1 and BIMS 301 in the same semester. Both are exam-heavy and their exam schedules often overlap. Take them sequentially. Visit OPSA no later than sophomore year to map out your full course sequence through graduation. Pre-med students who engage with OPSA early are significantly more likely to complete their prerequisite courses on time and maintain competitive GPAs. The professors who write the strongest recommendation letters are those who know you well — office hours attendance matters beyond exam preparation.

Content: Q&A summary from our Spring 2025 Course Planning Night. Most common question about Adam Barry — Yes he is worth waiting for on the waitlist. Every senior in the room agreed. He is genuinely one of the best professors in SPH and his recommendation letters are detailed and specific. Most common question about Joy DeLeon — Her upper division courses are reportedly better than HLTH 215. If you must take her consider HLTH 310 or 370 rather than 215 as your first exposure. Most common question about EPIB 301 — Take Clendenin. The workload is real but so is the preparation.

Source: TAMU Pre-Medical Society
Department: Pre-Med
Date: 2025-04-01
Content: Key lessons from our medical school panel with three TAMU alumni now in medical school. All three emphasized that the rigor of their undergraduate preparation mattered more than they expected. Herman's BIMS 301 came up by name from two of the three panelists as a course that directly prepared them for first-year medical school content. Goodey's Organic Chemistry was mentioned as the reason one panelist did not drop pre-med sophomore year. Barry's HLTH 320 was credited for giving one panelist a strong answer to interview questions about the social determinants of health.
